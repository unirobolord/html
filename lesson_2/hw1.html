Albert Einstein

Albert Einstein was a German-born theoretical physicist who developed the theory of relativity and is widely considered one of the most influential scientists of all time.

Early Life and Education

Albert Einstein was born on March 14, 1879, in Ulm, in the Kingdom of Württemberg, German Empire, to Jewish-agnostic parents Hermann Einstein and Pauline Koch.

He showed an early interest in science and mathematics, and he excelled in his studies. In 1900, he graduated from the Swiss Federal Polytechnic in Zurich with a degree in physics.

In 1905, he published four groundbreaking papers in the Annalen der Physik, which laid the foundation for modern physics and earned him a PhD from the University of Zurich.

In 1921, he was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics for his explanation of the photoelectric effect, which demonstrated the particle nature of light.

Einstein's work revolutionized our understanding of space, time, and energy, and his theories continue to shape the field of physics today.

Later Life and Legacy

Albert Einstein continued to work on theoretical physics throughout his life, and he made significant contributions to the development of quantum mechanics and cosmology.

He was also an outspoken advocate for peace and social justice, and he used his platform to speak out against war, racism, and oppression.

Albert Einstein passed away on April 18, 1955, in Princeton, New Jersey, at the age of 76. His legacy continues to inspire scientists and thinkers around the world, and his name has become synonymous with genius and innovation.
